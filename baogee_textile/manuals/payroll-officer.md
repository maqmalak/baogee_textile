# Payroll officer — structures and the monthly run

## Salary components
**Salary Components** holds every line of a salary slip:

| Earnings | Deductions |
|---|---|
| Basic, House Rent, Conveyance, Medical, Medical (Exempt), Utilities, Meal, Other Allowance, Overtime, Arrear, Leave Encashment | EOBI, Provident Fund, Income Tax, Attendance Deduction, Canteen, Advance Recovery, Loan Recovery, Other Deductions |

- Each component that posts to the books has an account per company (for example Basic → Salary; Advance and Loan Recovery → Employee Advances).

## Salary structures
1. **Salary Structures**: the formula for a group of staff, e.g. Basic = base, House Rent = 45% of Basic. Submit it.
2. **Structure Assignments**: give each employee a structure, the **base** (monthly pay) and the start date. Use **Bulk Salary Structure Assignment** for many at once.
3. An employee without an assignment gets no salary slip, and loan instalments can't be set up for them.

## Before the run
- Attendance complete for the month — payroll is **based on attendance**, and a day with no attendance counts as **absent**.
- Leave approved, overtime and arrears entered as **Additional Salary**.
- Advances and loans paid this month already show as deductions: loans as monthly instalments, cash advances in full.

## The run
1. **Payroll Entries → New**: company, month (start and end date), payroll payable account, cost centre, payment account.
2. **Get employees**, then submit: the salary slips are created as drafts.
3. Check **Salary Slips**: payment days, earnings, deductions, net pay. Fix attendance or additional salary and recreate if needed.
4. **Submit salary slips**: they are final and the payroll is posted to the accounts (salary expense, payroll payable, advance recovery).
5. **Make bank entry** when salaries are paid.

## After the run
- **Salary Loans**: this month's deductions against last month, outstanding balances, the loan ledger.
- Each loan's page shows its instalment schedule with the month deducted and the salary slip.
